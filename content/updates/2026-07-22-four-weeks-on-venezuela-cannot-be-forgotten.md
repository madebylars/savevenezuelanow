In La Guaira — the hardest hit state — 80% of buildings collapsed. Venezuela's seismic monitoring agency has now recorded over 1,100 aftershocks since June 24.</p><p>The world's news cycle has moved on. Venezuela hasn't.</p><p><strong>The scale of what remains</strong></p><p>Preliminary estimates put the total cost of reconstruction at $37 billion — roughly 30% of Venezuela's entire GDP. The UNDP has estimated $6.7 billion in direct physical damage alone. The United States has committed more than $300 million in humanitarian assistance. Venezuela's government announced a $200 million emergency reconstruction fund. Together those amounts cover less than 10% of what is needed.</p><p>Up to 6.8 million people — nearly a quarter of the country's remaining population — have been affected by the disaster, requiring shelter, water, sanitation, healthcare and other relief items, according to the UN's International Organization for Migration.</p><p><strong>From rescue to recovery — the long road ahead</strong></p><p>The search and rescue phase is over. What remains is harder and slower — the identification of the dead, the housing of the homeless, the rebuilding of destroyed infrastructure, and the long psychological recovery of a traumatised population.</p><p>Families in La Guaira are still paying up to $3,000 per day to hire cranes to recover the remains of loved ones from rubble. Hospitals that were already short of supplies before June 24 are now stretched beyond breaking point. Water systems have failed in some areas. Tens of thousands of people are living in temporary camps with no clear timeline for returning home.</p><p><strong>@MariaCorinaVM speaks</strong></p><p>Venezuela's Nobel Peace Prize-winning opposition leader María Corina Machado has repeatedly called on the international community not to abandon Venezuela in its hour of need. \"I know how each one of you feels,\" she said in the days after the earthquakes. \"Venezuela needs all of us.\"</p><p>She is right. The cameras may have gone. The need has not.</p><p><strong>World Vision, Direct Relief, and the organisations still there</strong></p><p>While media attention fades, humanitarian organisations are still on the ground.
