Venezuela's seismic monitoring agency recorded 1,115 earthquakes in just the first 336 hours after the disaster.</p><p>The UNDP has estimated $6.7 billion in direct physical damage — roughly 6% of Venezuela's entire GDP wiped out in 39 seconds. For a country that entered this disaster already in economic freefall, the scale of reconstruction is almost unimaginable.</p><p>The United States has committed more than $300 million in humanitarian assistance, and Venezuela's government announced a $200 million emergency reconstruction fund. Then killed.</strong></p><p>Among the most heartbreaking stories to emerge this week: a hotel in La Guaira that collapsed was housing recently deported Venezuelans — people who had arrived on government repatriation flights just hours before the earthquakes struck. Families in La Guaira have been told they must pay up to $3,000 per day to hire cranes to recover the remains of their loved ones from the rubble. For families who have already lost everything, this is devastating.</p><p>Venezuela has opened emergency mass grave sites to handle the volume of unidentified dead — a detail reported by El País that speaks to the sheer scale of what La Guaira has experienced.</p><p><strong>Spain mourns 40 of its own</strong></p><p>The international dimension of this tragedy continues to grow.
